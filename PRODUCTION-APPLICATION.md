## Part 1 — About your closed test

### 1. How easy was it to recruit testers for your app?

Pick the option that is true for you. Suggested: **Difficult** — as a solo
developer without an existing user base, most testers came through a
testing service.

### 2. Describe the engagement you received from testers during your closed test.

> 14-day test with 12 testers from a testing service plus friends and family. Testers installed from Play, added children and carers, planned a holiday week, copied sessions to other days and shared the week as a picture. The testing service's report on 25 October found no crashes or bugs.

*(288 characters)*

### 3. Provide a summary of the feedback you received from testers. Include how you collected it.

> Collected via a written report from the testing service, plus messages and in-app email feedback from testers I know. No crashes or bugs found. Suggestions: add a short "How it works" page, say on the welcome page that data stays on the phone, and explain the app better in the store listing.

*(292 characters)*

## Part 2 — About your app

### 4. Who is the intended audience of your app?

> Parents and guardians (18+) organising childcare in the school holidays, especially families sharing care between parents, grandparents, holiday clubs and childminders. It is for adults planning care, not for children to use.

*(225 characters)*

### 5. Describe how your app provides value to users.

> KidRota shows holiday weeks as a grid of who looks after each child and when, with gaps in the 8am–6pm day in red. Copy sessions across days and children, and share the week as a picture so family don't need the app. No account or ads; data stays on the phone. Pro (£1.99/yr): unlimited holidays.

*(296 characters)*

### 6. How many installs do you expect in your first year?

Suggested: **0 – 10K**. A new solo app with no marketing budget; pick a
higher range only if you have a plan that supports it.

## Part 3 — Your production readiness

### 7. What changes did you make to your app based on what you learned during your closed test?

> I rewrote the store listing and screenshots to explain the app more clearly. The next update (1.0.2) adds a "How it works" page and a line on the welcome page saying data stays on the phone. Version 1.0.1, used in the test, already had 18 fixes from my own internal testing.

*(274 characters)*

### 8. How did you decide that your app is ready for production?

> No crashes or bugs in the 14-day closed test. Before testing I did a full pre-release review (Play policy, privacy, billing, Android 14/15, accessibility) and fixed every serious issue. 291 automated tests pass. Billing uses Google Play, and the Data safety answers match the app.

*(280 characters)*

## After you submit

- Google says a decision usually takes **up to about 7 days**; you get an
  email and a message on the Dashboard.
- If approved: create a **Production** release (1.0.1 or 1.0.2). It does not
  go live by itself.
- If turned down: Google says why and may ask for more closed testing.
  Keep the closed test running until you hear back.
